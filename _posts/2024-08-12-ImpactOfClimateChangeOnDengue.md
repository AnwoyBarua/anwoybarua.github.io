Dengue fever, a mosquito-borne viral infection, has become a significant public health concern in many parts of the world. According to WHO, an estimated 100-400 million dengue infections occur yearly, putting half the population at risk. Bangladesh is also susceptible to dengue due to its tropical climate. Dengue incidences have drastically increased in Bangladesh over the years, with the previous year reporting 321,179 hospitalizations and 1,705 deaths, the highest in the nation's history.


While the spread of dengue fever is influenced by numerous factors, climate change stands out as a critical element that is reshaping the dynamics of this disease. The most important climatic factors linked to dengue transmission are temperature, rainfall, and relative humidity.

Usually at higher temperatures adult mosquitoes bite more frequently. Higher temperatures are suitable for dengue transmission as it affects the reproductive capacity of mosquitoes, stimulating egg hatching and accelerating growth of the larvae, thus decreasing the time to maturity. Warmer temperatures can extend the length of the season when mosquitoes are active, allowing a longer time period for dengue transmission.

Mosquitoes spend their whole life cycle in water before hatching into adult mosquitoes. Increased rainfall creates new habitat for larvae, and also ensures that water remains available for mosquito breeding over an extended period, increasing the overall lifespan and activity of the mosquito population. This leads to more opportunities for mosquitoes to bite humans and transmit the dengue virus. Furthermore, heavy rainfall leads to more indoor activities where Aedes mosquitoes, which prefer indoor environments, can easily bite humans. However, excessive rainfall can sometimes have a negative impact on mosquito populations by washing away larvae from breeding sites, temporarily reducing mosquito numbers.

A recent study shows that, when prior water availability is medium to low, heavy rainfall increases dengue risk by creating more mosquito habitat. Alongside, when prior water availability is high, heavy rainfall temporarily decreases the risk of dengue due to the flushing-out effect.

Dengue cases were reported more during monsoon months when relative humidity was higher because the higher humidity during the rainy season facilitates the growth and survival of infected mosquitoes for the successful propagation of the virus. High humidity increases the survival, feeding behavior, and egg development of Aedes mosquitoes. Hence, high humidity contributes to dengue incidence timespan and intensity due to mosquitoes prolonged lifespan.

Climate patterns in Bangladesh are changing dreadfully due to environmental pollution and the greenhouse effect, making the country increasingly suitable for the propagation of dengue and other vector-borne illnesses, including malaria and chikungunya.

As the climate crisis intensifies, the risk to public health grows more urgent and severe. By implementing robust public health interventions and sustainable environmental policies, we can mitigate the impacts of climate change on vector-borne diseases. It's crucial for Bangladesh and the global community to work together towards a healthier and more resilient future.
